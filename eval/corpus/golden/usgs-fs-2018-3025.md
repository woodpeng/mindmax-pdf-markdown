# Science Partnership Between U.S. Geological Survey and the Lower Elwha Klallam Tribe: Understanding the Elwha River Dam Removal Project

## The Elwha River Dam Removal and

## Ecosystem Restoration Project

After nearly a century of producing power, two large hydroelectric dams on the Elwha River in Washington State were removed during 2011 to 2014 to restore the river ecosystem and recover imperiled salmon populations. Roughly two-thirds of the 21 million cubic meters of sediment—enough to fill nearly 2 million dump trucks—contained behind the dams was released downstream, which restored natural processes and initiated important changes to the river, estuarine, and marine ecosystems. A multidisciplinary team of scientists from the Lower Elwha Klallam Tribe (LEKT), academia, nongovernmental organizations, Federal and state agencies, and the U.S. Geological Survey (USGS) collected key data before, during, and after dam removal to understand the outcomes of
